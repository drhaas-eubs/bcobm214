F65 · SLIBRARY 17 — PITCH ARCHITECTURE

Kawasaki's 10/20/30 Rule

Guy Kawasaki (2003)

Kawasaki's 10/20/30 Rule is a constraint-based discipline for investor pitch presentations: 10 slides as the maximum, 20 minutes as the maximum spoken duration, and 30 points as the minimum font size — three constraints designed to force focus, prevent over-loading and make the deck readable from the back of any room (Kawasaki, 2003).

10 slides · 20 minutes · 30-pt minimum font.

REFERENCE

Kawasaki, G. (2003) The 10/20/30 Rule of PowerPoint. guykawasaki.com, December.

F66 · SLIBRARY 17 — PITCH ARCHITECTURE

Sequoia's Pitch Deck Template

Sequoia Capital (2010s)

The Sequoia Pitch Deck Template is a 10-slide structure published by Sequoia Capital that orders the questions sophisticated investors weigh — Purpose, Problem, Solution, Why Now, Market Size, Competition, Product, Business Model, Team, Financials — with each slide answering one core question and the order chosen so that earlier slides set context for later ones (Sequoia Capital, 2010s).

Purpose · Problem · Solution · Why now · Market · Competition · Product · Business model · Team · Financials.

REFERENCE

Sequoia Capital (2010s) Writing a Business Plan. sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan.

F67 · SLIBRARY 17 — PITCH ARCHITECTURE

Heath Brothers' SUCCESs

Chip & Dan Heath (2007)

SUCCESs is a six-attribute checklist for designing memorable messages, derived from analysis of why some ideas spread while others die: Simple (one core message), Unexpected (break a pattern to gain attention), Concrete (sensory specificity), Credible (with evidence or details), Emotional (felt, not just understood), and Stories (told as narrative rather than abstraction) (Heath & Heath, 2007).

Simple · Unexpected · Concrete · Credible · Emotional · Stories.

REFERENCE

Heath, C. & Heath, D. (2007) Made to Stick: Why Some Ideas Survive and Others Die. New York: Random House.

F68 · SLIBRARY 17 — PITCH ARCHITECTURE

Duarte's Resonate Pattern

Nancy Duarte (2010)

Duarte's Resonate Pattern is a structural model for inspirational presentations and speeches in which the speaker oscillates repeatedly between current reality ('what is') and future possibility ('what could be'), with each oscillation building emotional momentum toward a final 'new bliss' state — a structure observed across great speeches from Aristotle to Steve Jobs (Duarte, 2010).

Oscillating between 'what is' and 'what could be'.

REFERENCE

Duarte, N. (2010) Resonate: Present Visual Stories That Transform Audiences. Hoboken, NJ: Wiley.

F69 · SLIBRARY 18 — DELIVERY CRAFT

Mehrabian's 7-38-55

Albert Mehrabian (1971)

Mehrabian's 7-38-55 is the empirical finding that when verbal, vocal and visual channels conflict in expressing emotion or attitude, listeners weight body language (55%) and vocal tone (38%) more heavily than the words themselves (7%) — applicable specifically to incongruent affective messages, not all communication, but commonly generalised in pitch coaching to emphasise the importance of channel alignment (Mehrabian, 1971).

Words 7% · Tone 38% · Body language 55% — when channels conflict.

REFERENCE

Mehrabian, A. (1971) Silent Messages: Implicit Communication of Emotions and Attitudes. Belmont, CA: Wadsworth.

F70 · SLIBRARY 18 — DELIVERY CRAFT

Aristotle's Ethos · Pathos · Logos

Aristotle (~350 BCE)

Aristotle's three modes of persuasion (the pisteis) are Ethos (the speaker's perceived credibility, character and authority), Pathos (the appeal to the audience's emotions and values), and Logos (the logical structure of the argument and its supporting evidence) — together identified in Rhetoric (~350 BCE) as the necessary and sufficient elements of effective persuasion (Aristotle, ~350 BCE).

Credibility · Emotion · Logic — three modes of persuasion.

REFERENCE

Aristotle (~350 BCE) Rhetoric. (Translated by Roberts, W. R., 1924; available in Loeb Classical Library editions.)

F71 · SLIBRARY 18 — DELIVERY CRAFT

Anderson's TED Commandments

Chris Anderson (2016)

Anderson's TED Commandments are six principles distilled from two decades of curating TED Talks: a single Throughline that holds the entire talk together; an opening Curiosity hook; the use of Story to anchor abstraction; a Power of Explanation that makes complex things clear; Emotional honesty without manipulation; and ruthless Brevity (Anderson, 2016).

Throughline · Curiosity · Story · Power of explanation · Emotion · Brevity.

REFERENCE

Anderson, C. (2016) TED Talks: The Official TED Guide to Public Speaking. Boston: Houghton Mifflin Harcourt.

F72 · SLIBRARY 18 — DELIVERY CRAFT

Cuddy's Power Posing

Amy Cuddy (2010, 2012 TED)

Power Posing is the practice of adopting expansive, open postures (e.g., arms raised, chest out, hands on hips) for two minutes before high-stakes events, on the basis of research suggesting the posture shifts both observer perception and self-perception, reducing cortisol and improving confidence-related performance — though the original neuroendocrine claims have been contested in subsequent replication studies (Cuddy et al., 2010; Carney & Cuddy retracted neuroendocrine claims, 2016).

Two minutes of expansive posture before high-stakes moments.

REFERENCE

Carney, D. R., Cuddy, A. J. C. & Yap, A. J. (2010) Power Posing: Brief Nonverbal Displays Affect Neuroendocrine Levels and Risk Tolerance. Psychological Science, 21(10), pp. 1363–1368.
