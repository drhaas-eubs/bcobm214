F33 · SLIBRARY 9 — PLAN ARCHITECTURE

Sahlman's Great Business Plan Frame

William Sahlman (1997)

Sahlman's Great Business Plan Frame is a four-element analytical structure for writing or evaluating any new-venture business plan: People (the team and what they bring), Opportunity (the market and how it will be captured), Context (the macro and regulatory environment), and Risk & Reward (the deal's distribution of outcomes) (Sahlman, 1997).

People · Opportunity · Context · Risk & Reward.

REFERENCE

Sahlman, W. A. (1997) How to Write a Great Business Plan. Harvard Business Review, Jul–Aug, pp. 98–108.

F34 · SLIBRARY 9 — PLAN ARCHITECTURE

SMART Goals (recap)

George Doran (1981)

SMART (recap) is the application of Doran's five-attribute goal-test (introduced as F8) to the milestones section of the business plan, ensuring every plan milestone is operationally usable rather than aspirational language (Doran, 1981).

Specific · Measurable · Achievable · Relevant · Time-bound.

REFERENCE

Doran, G. T. (1981) There's a S.M.A.R.T. Way to Write Management's Goals and Objectives. Management Review, 70(11), pp. 35–36.

F35 · SLIBRARY 9 — PLAN ARCHITECTURE

OKR (Objectives and Key Results)

Andy Grove (1970s) · John Doerr (1999 onward)

OKR is a goal-setting and tracking framework in which the organisation sets a small number of qualitative Objectives (what we want to achieve) and supports each with 3–5 quantitative Key Results (how we will measure progress), reviewed on a quarterly cycle with cascading alignment from company to team to individual (Doerr, 2018).

Objectives (qualitative) + Key Results (quantitative).

REFERENCE

Doerr, J. (2018) Measure What Matters. New York: Portfolio.

F36 · SLIBRARY 9 — PLAN ARCHITECTURE

Kaplan & Norton's Balanced Scorecard

Robert Kaplan & David Norton (1992)

The Balanced Scorecard is a strategic performance-management framework that translates an organisation's mission and strategy into a balanced set of objectives and measures across four perspectives — Financial, Customer, Internal Process, and Learning & Growth — linked through cause-and-effect logic (Kaplan & Norton, 1992).

Financial · Customer · Internal Process · Learning & Growth.

REFERENCE

Kaplan, R. S. & Norton, D. P. (1992) The Balanced Scorecard — Measures That Drive Performance. Harvard Business Review, Jan–Feb, pp. 71–79.

F37 · SLIBRARY 10 — PITFALLS & PLAN QUALITY

Eisenmann's 6 Patterns of Startup Failure

Tom Eisenmann (2021)

Eisenmann's Six Patterns is an empirical typology of recurring startup failure modes — Bad Bedfellows (founder-investor misalignment), False Start (skipping customer discovery), False Promise (misreading early traction), Help Wanted (failure to build the team), Cascading Miracles (multi-step plans that require everything to go right), and Speed Trap (over-investment in growth before unit economics work) — derived from a study of hundreds of failed ventures (Eisenmann, 2021).

Bad Bedfellows · False Start · False Promise · Help Wanted · Cascading Miracles · Speed Trap.

REFERENCE

Eisenmann, T. R. (2021) Why Startups Fail. New York: Currency.

F38 · SLIBRARY 10 — PITFALLS & PLAN QUALITY

Bhide's Hustle as Strategy

Amar Bhide (1986)

Hustle as Strategy is the contention that in industries with low entry barriers and high uncertainty — typical of most early-stage ventures — sustainable competitive advantage rarely comes from strategic positioning. It comes instead from sheer execution velocity: out-iterating, out-shipping, out-hustling competitors faster than they can respond (Bhide, 1986).

For early ventures, execution velocity often beats strategic perfection.

REFERENCE

Bhide, A. (1986) Hustle as Strategy. Harvard Business Review, Sep–Oct, pp. 59–65.

F39 · SLIBRARY 10 — PITFALLS & PLAN QUALITY

Christensen's Innovator's Dilemma

Clayton Christensen (1997)

The Innovator's Dilemma is the systemic vulnerability of successful incumbent firms to low-end or new-market disruption: rational allocation of resources to high-margin customers — exactly the customers least interested in disruptive innovations — predictably blinds incumbents to entrants who target underserved segments and then move upmarket (Christensen, 1997).

Doing everything right for current customers blinds firms to disruptive entrants.

REFERENCE

Christensen, C. M. (1997) The Innovator's Dilemma. Boston: Harvard Business School Press.

F40 · SLIBRARY 10 — PITFALLS & PLAN QUALITY

North Star Metric

Sean Ellis · GrowthHackers (~2010s)

A North Star Metric is a single quantitative measure that captures the core value the product delivers to customers and predicts long-term revenue, used as the anchor of operational dashboards, prioritisation decisions and team alignment (Ellis, ~2010s; Burnes & Lessin, 2018).

The one number that captures the core value delivered.

REFERENCE

Burnes, J. & Lessin, S. (2018) The North Star Playbook. Amplitude.
