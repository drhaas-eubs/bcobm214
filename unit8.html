F57 · SLIBRARY 15 — SOURCES OF CAPITAL

Bootstrapping Ladder

Industry practice — Greg Gianforte (2005) et al.

The Bootstrapping Ladder is a stage-sequenced model of funding sources used by founders building businesses without external equity capital, ascending from personal savings and sweat equity through customer pre-payments, supplier credit, factoring and asset-light service revenue, with each rung preserving founder ownership and operational control (industry practice; Gianforte, 2005).

Personal savings → revenue financing → trade credit → asset-light scaling.

REFERENCE

Gianforte, G. (2005) Bootstrapping Your Business: Start-Up to $200M+ Without Investors. Avon, MA: Adams Media.

F58 · SLIBRARY 15 — SOURCES OF CAPITAL

Friends, Family & Fools (FFF)

Industry idiom (1990s onward)

Friends, Family & Fools (FFF) is the colloquial label for the earliest external capital raised by most founders — small amounts, informal terms, drawn from personal networks, with the 'fools' being early investors willing to back unproven ventures on weak documentation. FFF capital is typically the only capital available before institutional rounds (industry idiom; Cassar, 2004).

The earliest external capital — informal, fast, emotionally expensive.

REFERENCE

Cassar, G. (2004) The Financing of Business Start-Ups. Journal of Business Venturing, 19(2), pp. 261–283.

F59 · SLIBRARY 15 — SOURCES OF CAPITAL

Crowdfunding Models

Mollick (2014) · Kickstarter, Indiegogo, Crowdcube

Crowdfunding is the practice of funding a venture through small contributions from a large number of people via internet platforms, with four distinct sub-models: Reward (pre-purchase products with no equity), Equity (small investors receive shares), Debt / Lending (peer-to-peer loans with interest), and Donation (no financial return) — each with distinct regulatory, motivational and economic profiles (Mollick, 2014).

Reward · Equity · Debt · Donation — four engines, four logics.

REFERENCE

Mollick, E. (2014) The Dynamics of Crowdfunding: An Exploratory Study. Journal of Business Venturing, 29(1), pp. 1–16.

F60 · SLIBRARY 15 — SOURCES OF CAPITAL

Angel · VC · PE Spectrum

Industry practice

The Angel·VC·PE Spectrum is a typology of equity-investor categories distinguished by typical cheque size, stage, control rights and hold period: Angels (€25k–€500k, idea-to-seed, light involvement, 8–10 year exit), Venture Capital (€500k–€100m+, seed-to-late-stage, board involvement, 7–10 year fund cycle), and Private Equity (€10m+, growth-to-buyout, control or significant minority, 5–7 year hold) (industry practice).

Cheque size, stage, control, hold period — picking the right capital partner.

REFERENCE

Gompers, P. & Lerner, J. (2001) The Venture Capital Cycle. 2nd ed. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press.

F61 · SLIBRARY 16 — INVESTMENT STAGES & TERMS

Funding Curve

Industry data — Kauffman, CB Insights, Crunchbase

The Funding Curve is an empirical typology of the standard sequence of equity rounds for venture-funded companies — Pre-Seed, Seed, Series A, B, C, Growth, IPO/Exit — each with characteristic milestones, valuations, dilution and time-to-next-round, allowing founders to position their company on the curve and reverse-engineer the work needed for the next round (industry data; CB Insights, Pitchbook).

Pre-Seed · Seed · Series A · B · C · Growth · IPO/Exit.

REFERENCE

CB Insights (2024) State of Venture Report. cbinsights.com.

F62 · SLIBRARY 16 — INVESTMENT STAGES & TERMS

Capitalization Table

Industry practice

A Capitalization Table (cap table) is the source-of-truth record of equity ownership in a company, listing each shareholder's holdings (common, preferred, options, warrants, convertible instruments) and updating with every funding event, option grant or equity transaction; it is the document founders, investors, lawyers and acquirers reference for ownership decisions (industry practice).

Who owns what · Founder dilution · Option pool · Pre/post-money math.

REFERENCE

Feld, B. & Mendelson, J. (2019) Venture Deals. 4th ed. Hoboken, NJ: Wiley.

F63 · SLIBRARY 16 — INVESTMENT STAGES & TERMS

Pre-Money / Post-Money Valuation

VC industry standard

Pre-Money Valuation is the value assigned to a company before a financing round; Post-Money Valuation equals Pre-Money plus the new Investment amount; the investor's ownership percentage equals Investment divided by Post-Money — the most-confused but most-consequential calculation in early-stage finance, materially affected by where the option pool is created (in pre-money, founders dilute; in post-money, investors dilute) (VC industry standard).

Pre + Investment = Post. Investor's % = Investment ÷ Post.

REFERENCE

Feld, B. & Mendelson, J. (2019) Venture Deals. 4th ed. Hoboken, NJ: Wiley.

F64 · SLIBRARY 16 — INVESTMENT STAGES & TERMS

Y Combinator SAFE

Carolynn Levy / Y Combinator (2013)

The Y Combinator SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity) is a financing instrument that converts to equity at a future priced round at either a pre-set valuation cap or a discount (whichever is more favourable to the investor), with no interest, no maturity date, and no legal entitlement to convert if no priced round occurs — designed to simplify and accelerate early-stage financing (Y Combinator, 2013, updated 2018).

Simple Agreement for Future Equity — no interest, no maturity, valuation cap and discount.

REFERENCE

Y Combinator (2013, updated 2018) The SAFE: Simple Agreement for Future Equity. ycombinator.com/documents.
